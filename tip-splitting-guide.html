How to split tips fairly: the 3 methods that actually work

Every tip argument comes down to one thing: the method wasn't written down before the money hit the jar. Here are the three splitting methods bars and restaurants actually use — with worked examples — and the situations where each one is the right answer.

Method 1 — Straight hours split

Formula: payout = tips × (your hours ÷ total team hours).

Example: $1,000 pool. Anna 40h, Ben 30h, Carla 25h, Diego 40h → 135 total hours. Anna: 40/135 × $1,000 = $296.30.

Use when: everyone does roughly the same job (all servers, all bartenders). It's the simplest defensible split — nobody can argue with arithmetic.

Weakness: a 6-hour shift-closer earns the same hourly rate as the person who did the lunch rush.

Method 2 — Role-weighted points

Formula: points = hours × role weight; payout = tips × (your points ÷ total points).

Common weight sets: server 1.0, bartender 1.0, barback 0.7, kitchen 0.7, busser 0.6, host 0.5. With the same crew above (Carla the busser at 0.6, Diego kitchen at 0.7), a $1,000 pool pays Anna $353.98 and Carla $132.74 — support roles still share, but weighted.

Use when: mixed roles contribute differently and you want the split to show it — without building a whole policy.

Method 3 — FOH/BOH pools (the policy method)

Formula: each group gets a fixed % of the pool (e.g. 60% FOH / 40% BOH), then that slice splits by hours within the group.

This is how most written tip policies work, because it's easy to explain to staff and stable week to week. $1,000 pool, 60/40, Anna+Ben in FOH (70h), Diego alone in BOH: Anna $342.86, Ben $257.14, Diego $400. Kitchen-in-the-pool shops typically run 70/30 to 80/20 FOH/BOH on card tips.

Use when: you have front + back of house sharing tips and want the ratio set by policy, not by who happened to clock more hours.

Legal quick-check (US)

  • Federal rules let employers run tip pools only among employees who receive tips — managers/supervisors can't share (many states extend this further).
  • Tip credits (using tips toward minimum wage) interact with pooling — check your state before changing method mid-year.
  • California, Washington and others restrict employer involvement entirely; a written method + payout records (like the printable sheet from the calculator) is your best protection in disputes.

One rule that beats all methods

Whatever method you pick: post the method, show the math, keep the records. 90% of tip disputes end the moment the payout sheet on the wall shows the pool, the method, and every payout to the cent.
